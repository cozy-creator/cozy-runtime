A sharded stage runs at its group's degree or on one GPU, chosen at its first sharded call by its key: the package build, function, stage, that call's video shape exactly and its other tensors (a prompt's text rows) to the next power of two, card, degree and Runtime build. Two GPUs should simply be faster, so a key runs at the group's degree until measured otherwise, and wherever nothing can be measured (no kernel cache, a stage of one sharded call). The group's degree is measured first, then one GPU: one GPU runs each sharded call unsharded on the group GPU with the room and its output crosses, while the others run the rest of the stage's program on it and compute, fetch and hold none of the call. A measurement whose steps loaded weights, built or landed a kernel or recorded a graph is cold, and is measured once more, warm, then kept. One GPU is chosen only where it is more than 5% faster (Paul 11-15: within 5% is a tie, and a tie goes to the group's degree). Where one GPU is known first, the group's degree is tried against its second step, and one more than 5% slower moves the rest of that stage to one GPU. The machine records each degree's steady and second step and the attention kernels it served; each stage's row in `transport.stages` names its key, its degree and, where so, `hosted`, `home` and `trial_ms` (cr-348 C).
