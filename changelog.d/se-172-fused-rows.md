A model's RMS norms run in one kernel a call, with the scale and shift its block applies after them (`norm(x) * (1 + scale) + shift`), and the rotations its attention processors apply to queries and keys (split or interleaved pairs, fp32 tables read where they lie) run in one kernel too, wherever its own code is written that way: nothing is declared and no package changes. Each norm, and each rotation's shape of call, proves the kernel against its own result on its first call, within the rounding the two computations may differ by, on the card and with no wait; the verdict is kept in the machine's kernel store, so a later process recalls it and runs no comparison. A norm that is one kernel already, or another law, is left as written. Each run's record says the checks it ran (`kernel.fused_rows`). LTX-2.5's denoise steps spent most of their elementwise time in these norms and rotations (se-172).
