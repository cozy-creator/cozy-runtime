`VideoProbe.frame_rate` is the frame clock a video's packets keep, not its header's average. An ffmpeg stream-copy join of a 24 fps take and a 24 fps clip with B-frames averaged 20232/841 (24.057) with every frame 1/24 long, so MiniMax H3's and LTX 2.5's retakes and pickups warned that the take wasn't 24 fps and mapped their window onto a clock that drifts a frame every 17 s. On a time base too coarse for the rate (Matroska's milliseconds at 30000/1001), the clock is the stated rate every frame is within a tick of. Frames on no clock set the new `VideoProbe.variable_frame_rate`, with `frame_rate` the average (the header's, else the frames'), never a rounded rate. One rule (`frame_clock`) gives every reader its clock: the probe, a decode's header (the rate `save_video_stream` writes a pass-through at), `splice_video`'s and the packet-copy joins' fixed-clock checks, and the output probe. The probe reads the video's packet times; it still decodes nothing (se-177).

`splice_video` starts its decode clock early enough for every copied picture to decode by its time, so a join of a track without B-frames and one with them (a Runtime take and an ffmpeg clip) splices instead of refusing `its pictures reorder further than its decode clock allows`. A source it spliced before gets the same clock.
