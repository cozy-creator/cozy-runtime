Checkpoint uploads size their push window with TensorFS's `transfer_streams(16)`, so a small pod pushes fewer objects at once instead of assuming a RAM floor; grants are fetched two windows ahead, and a queued grant that goes cold is asked for again instead of being sent expired. A sent upload whose finalize Hub never received now follows the same stall rule as an unsent one: when Hub refuses to stage it again, or ten attempts land nothing, a fresh status read that still finds no finalize settles it uncommitted and releases its hold, instead of leaving it pending until the run is canceled. Upload retries and reconcile polls no longer re-retain or lease the whole closure: the first attempt retains it, each push leases only its own object and re-checks the hold, and Host-driven byte-tree transfers do the same. The source worker always passes TensorFS's download progress callback (TensorFS >= 0.3.59).
