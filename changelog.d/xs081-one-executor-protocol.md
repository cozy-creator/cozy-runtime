The executor reads TensorD's commands and answers and builds its requests through `tensorfs.executor`, the classes compiled from the Rust definitions TensorD uses; the Runtime's own copies (`executor_commands.py`, `_executor_requests.py`) are gone. The protocol loses what nothing sent: the `activate`, `unload`, `probe`, `shutdown` and `capability` commands (a request's construction is the one it names), `Start.model_choices`, `Hello.request_lookahead` and `memory_backend`, and `Binding`'s `adapters`, `prepared_use`, `variant`, `placement`, `development`, `custody` and its singular/plural pairs. A load names `models: [Binding]` only. Needs a TensorD and TensorFS wheel of the same change: an older machine's commands are refused (xs-081).
